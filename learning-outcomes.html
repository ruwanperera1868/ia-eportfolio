Learning Outcomes

The module’s four learning outcomes, and where this portfolio evidences each one. LO1 is analysing agent architectures, LO2 is applying agent techniques under risk and uncertainty, LO3 is deploying tools with legal, social, ethical and professional awareness, and LO4 is working effectively in a virtual development team.

1. Identify and critically analyse agent-based systems, differentiating between architectures and approaches

Evidence What it shows
Unit 1 Simple reflex against model-based agents: memory cut steps from 390.5 to 34.9 in a 20-cell corridor
Unit 3 Subsumption against BDI: 8.8 against 35.0 packages, and why reactive control stalled
Unit 4 A hybrid architecture, and how its layers interacted badly until the battery reserve was tuned
Team work The design’s BDI mapping and the choice of LangGraph over CrewAI
Key concepts Definitions tied to each experiment

2. Apply and critically evaluate intelligent agent techniques to real-world problems, particularly where technical risk and uncertainty is involved

Evidence What it shows
Final project An LLM agent applied to literature review, with nine faults found by testing and live runs (R1 to R9)
Unit 10 Hidden error on a minority group behind a low overall error rate
Unit 11 How adaptive agents produce market instability that no single agent intends
Case study 2 Bounded autonomy as a response to uncertainty in an LLM’s judgement

3. Deploy critically appropriate software tools and skills for the design and implementation of an agent-based system, bearing in mind applicable legal, social, ethical and professional issues

Evidence What it shows
Final project LangGraph, Pydantic, SQLite, Flask and a locally run Qwen model; 83 tests at 96% coverage
Units 2, 5, 7, 8 and 9 Working code for logic, KQML messaging, word embeddings, parsing and neural networks
Case studies 1 and 3 Bias in retrieval rules, explainability, and the EU AI Act’s transparency duties
Home page A declaration of how AI tools were used in this work

4. Systematically develop and implement the skills required to be an effective member of a development team in a virtual professional environment, adopting real-life perspectives on team roles and organisation

Evidence What it shows
Team work How Group F, working across countries, moved from an edge-SLM thesis planner to the Evidence Investigator
